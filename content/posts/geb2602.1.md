# GEB 2602  Material Science

## Overview

**Processing** Decides **Structure** Decides **Property** Decides **Performance**

A material is some substance/mixture of it used to make something else.

| Material                 | 核心特点                                        | 常见应用               |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Metals（金属）           | Conductive, Ductile, **Tough but not hard**     | Bridges, wires         |
| Ceramics（陶瓷）         | **Hard but not tough**, Brittle, Heat-resistant | Glass, porcelain       |
| Polymers（聚合物）       | Lightweight, Flexible                           | Plastic, rubber        |
| Composites（复合材料）   | Combine different properties                    | Concrete, carbon fiber |
| Semiconductors（半导体） | Controllable conductivity                       | Chips, sensors         |

**Hardness (硬度)**：抗压入/划伤/塑性变形

**Strength (强度)**：抗非弹性弯折变形 Plastic Deformation

**Toughness (韧性)**：抗断裂 (不一定抗弯折)，反义: **brittleness**

**Stiffness (刚度)**：抗弹性变形 Elastic Deformation

**Fatigue**: Repeated Loading

## Paper

Paper is not a uniform solid film. It is a three-dimensional **porous fibre network**.

Wood fibers contains:

- **Cellulose**: long chained polymer, **stable, colorless**, reflects light well

  Basis of white paper: **Rich in –OH groups**, forms **Hydorgen Bonds**, which provides strength of paper.

  Wet paper are weaker: **H<sub>2</sub>O molecules enter fibre network and interfere with fibre-fibre hydrogen bonding**.

  Cotton paper is mainly cellulose so it's strong (e.g. bank notes)

- **Lignin**: brown, **reactive** polymer, gives wood rigidity, **photo-oxidize to turn yellow**. Acid and light ages paper. Gives the smell of paper.

A resource for paper is **GOOD** if contains much **Cellulose**

</br>

Use **pulping to extract fibers**:

- **Mechanical Pulping**: Preserves 95% of Lignin, Weak and prone to photo-oxidization. **Newspaper**
- **Chemical Pulpiing**: Preserves long fibres of Cellulose, de-lignin, strong and stable. **Kraft Paper**

</br>

Beating & Refining: More contact area → **More hydrogen bonds → Stronger paper**. Overtreating can hurt the fibre structure and **lower Porosity**

</br>

**Anisotropy**: different properties in differed directions (due to fiber orientation)

- Machine Direction 平行于纤维 (造纸机器): Aligned Fibres, higher tensil strength 抗拉强度, straighter tearing 因为裂纹可能沿纤维之间传播
- Cross Direction 垂直于纤维: Less Aligned, lower tensil strength, irreguler tearing
- Fibres tend to **align along the machine direction**. 抗拉强度与抗撕裂强度无关

</br>

Porous: multi-holed (pores). Porosity = **Fraction of empty volumn** inside a material.

- Higher porosity → Lower density, better air permeability/water absorption, lower strength, higher drying speed.
- Tissue paper 吸水性强: **porous network on its surface** for water to absorp + **Capillary Action 毛细作用**；while Coated Paper (涂布纸) 表面较致密

</br>

Corrugated Board (瓦楞纸板): **Structure can improve performance without changing the material itself.**

- **Sandwich Structure** increases the **second moment of area/cross-section and therefore bending stiffness**.

3R: reduce, reuse, recycle

</br>

### Insights

**Q1. Why does wet paper tear more easily?**

Water weakens hydrogen bonding between cellulose fibres, reducing the strength of the fibre network.

**Q2. Why is newspaper more likely to turn yellow than kraft paper?**

Mechanical pulping retains more lignin, which undergoes photo-oxidation and contributes to yellowing.

**Q3. Why does paper have different strengths in different directions?**

Fibres tend to align along the machine direction, creating anisotropic mechanical properties.

**Q4. Why is corrugated board stiffer than flat paper?**

Its sandwich-like structure increases the second moment of area and therefore bending stiffness.

## Ceramics

Keywords: Inorganic, Non-metallic and Solid. Inert, hard, high m.p., high stiffness.

⚠️ Strong bonding → High hardness, **but not necessarily high toughness.** High Elastic Modulus.

Ceramics are either **ionic or covalent**. Unlike a sea of elctrons in that of metals, forming current within their free electrons, thus conducting electricity.

- **High bond energy**, therefore **hard, high m.t.**

- **Ionic**: nondirectional bonding, but cleavage on crystal planes. Transfered electron, like-charge repulsion

- **Covalent**: directional bonding, shared electron, fixed bond angles

- Ionicity is a **continuum/proportion instead of an acurate classification**

  Al₂O₃ 约 67% ionic；Si₃N₄ 约 28% ionic；SiC 约 10% ionic

Coordination Number: how many anions a given cation attracts around it

</br>

Clay sintering (烧结) is irreversible, putting it in water does not soften it. **Higher bond energy requires higher sintering temp**. Sintering is **not necessarily at m.p.**, to **diffuse atoms, not to melt**.

Production of ceramics: Powder → Forming → Drying → Firing → Finishing

Ceramics cannot bend because unlike metals, its atoms cannot shuffle position without breaking, Dislocation motion is difficult:

- charge repulsion when trying to slide and shuffle atoms

- directed bond (covalent) WILL break
- Few **slip systems**

</br>

⚠️ 陶瓷 Pottery and Porcelain: the latter is smoother, translucent. The former has lower sintering/firing temp. and porous.

⚠️ 钢铁 Steel and Iron: the former is mixture (alloy).

</br>

### Insights

**Q1. Why are ceramics hard but brittle?**

Strong ionic and covalent bonds provide high hardness, but restrict dislocation motion and plastic deformation. As a result, cracks can propagate without significant plastic energy dissipation.

**Q2. Why does increasing porosity reduce ceramic strength?**

Pores reduce the effective load-bearing area and act as stress concentrators, promoting crack initiation and propagation.

**Q3. Why is sintering irreversible?**

Sintering involves atomic diffusion and the formation of strong bonds between particles, producing a consolidated structure that cannot simply be reversed by adding water.

**Q4. If crack size increases four times, what happens to fracture strength?**

According to Griffith's relationship \[\sigma_f\propto 1/\sqrt c\]

Therefore, fracture strength decreases to half its original value.

**Q5. What is the difference between hardness and fracture toughness?**

Hardness measures resistance to local indentation or scratching, while fracture toughness measures resistance to crack propagation.

## Metal

Iron: Soft (scratched by nail) and **Malleable**, relatively week

delocalized electron sea——conductive, malleable, non-directional bonding

plastic deformation: under stress atoms rearranged, while ceramic breaks

BCC 体心立方：顶点+正方体心

FCC 面心立方：顶点+六面面心

HCP 六方最密：正四面体，各向异性

铁在升温时 (910C) 从 BCC 到 FCC，可逆

Carbon is at interstitial (离最近铁原子最远空隙处)，提高硬度

Chromium：>11% 保护性氧化膜——不锈钢

镍：stabilizes austenite, improves toughness

锰、钼、钒：形成碳化物提高硬度

FCC 可以溶解更多的碳原子 (2.1%) 而 BCC 更少 0.02%

**Steel 的碳原子含量低于 ⚠️ 2.14%，更高就是铸铁 cast iron (便宜、脆 brittle)**

铸铁常含有 graphite 石墨/cementite 黑色区域，熔点低易铸造脆性高难锻造 (forge)

​    Grey Cast iron：flake graphite 对拉力/压力脆弱，可以减震

​    Ductile Cast iron：spherical graphite 

钢铁碳含量更低 Ductile。

</br>

**Q1. Why is pure iron soft but steel much harder?**

Carbon atoms occupy interstitial sites and distort the iron lattice, hindering dislocation motion and increasing hardness.

**Q2. Why can Austenite dissolve more carbon than Ferrite?**

Austenite has an FCC structure with interstitial sites that can accommodate more carbon than the BCC structure of ferrite.

**Q3. What is Pearlite?**

Pearlite is a lamellar microstructure consisting of alternating ferrite and cementite layers.

**Q4. Why does Quenching produce hard steel?**

Rapid cooling suppresses carbon diffusion and forms martensite, where trapped carbon distorts the lattice and restricts dislocation motion.

**Q5. Why is Tempering necessary after Quenching?**

Tempering relieves internal stresses, reduces brittleness and improves toughness, usually at the cost of some hardness.

**Q6. Why is Ductile Cast Iron tougher than Grey Cast Iron?**

Spherical graphite causes less stress concentration than flake graphite, improving ductility and tensile strength.

**Q7. Why does stainless steel resist corrosion?**

Chromium forms a thin, protective Cr₂O₃ passive layer on the steel surface, limiting further corrosion.

## Glass

Prism：disperse light (white - rainbow/spectrum)

Glass is an amorphous supercool liquid

石英 (晶体) 有一个固定 sharp 熔点 T<sub>m</sub>，melts abruptly；

玻璃 (非晶体) 只有 gradual transition 开始点 T<sub>g</sub>；T<sub>g</sub> 以下是 high viscosity (粘度)/坚硬的，以上是流动的**过冷却液体**；在一个 broad range 上随着升温逐渐软化 (can be blown or shaped)，T<sub>g</sub> 不是相比，不会有体积突变。

晶体是宏观上重复规则的 (regular lattice)，非晶体只有局部规则 (amorphous)

Zachariasen's Network **Trio**：SiO₂、B₂O₃、P₂O₅ 等氧化物由于其 flexibility 更容易作为玻璃 **Formers**；Na₂O, K₂O, CaO 作为 **Modifiers** (Break network bonds 以降低前者熔点)；Al₂O₃ 作为 **Intermediates** (提升 durability)。

SiO<sub>4</sub> Tetrahedron Network Flexibility：可以以等键角组成晶体，也可以与其他氧化物共熔为不规则氧化物。

</br>

**Why is glass transparant?**

玻璃中电子有两个可以处于的能级 energy levels，有一定能量带隙：**valence band-conduction band gap**。光子 photon 的能量不足以激发跃迁 transition/elevation，因此光子直接穿过所以是透明的；但玻璃对高能量 UV 可以吸收 (短波紫外线 UVb 可以被吸收，长波 UVa 不行)

金属-电子海，任意能量的光子都可以触发 (**no gap**)，所以光子被**absorbed or reflected**：**opaque and shiny**

**Why Does a Straw Look Bent in Water?**

玻璃中光传播速度低于空气，导致折射 refraction：lenses 的原理。Lead Glass is sparkler

**What Gives Glass Its Color?**

绿色：沙子中常见杂质 Fe<sup>2+</sup>，便宜。玻璃窗/瓶

红色：gold nanoparticles，吸收蓝绿段

蓝色：Co(II)，吸收红橙段

琥珀色：Fe<sup>3+</sup> sulfur，阻挡 UV，啤酒瓶/药瓶防止光解

</br>

自然玻璃：Fulgurites (petrified lightning)，Libyan Desert Glass

玻璃窗：70% Quarz、15% Soda (Sodium Carbonate) 作为 flux 降低熔点 (Quarz-1700 to 1000 C)、10% Limestone (Calcium Carbonate) 没有碳酸钙的钠玻璃会在水中溶解

**Float Process**: Pour molten glass onto liquid tin, spreads flat. Flat glass costs 90% less then before.

</br>

相比便宜受热易碎的 soda-lime glass (Heats unevenly)，Pyrex (Borosillicate Glass) 含 B₂O₃ 对 thermal shock 更 resistent (Less heat expansion)。

**Why Does Car Side Window Glass Shatter into Cubes?**

Tempered glass 被快速加热冷却 (heated and air-cooled/**quenched** rapidly)，表面先收缩并变得致密；内部保持受压状态 (Surface compressed, inside in tension) —— 5x stronger

Rupert's Drop：被冷水快速冷却，头强尾弱

</br>

Griffith Theory：tiny scratches under tension, stress concentrates at the crack tip，**Strong in compression, weak in tension**

Laminated：polymer holds cracks，Fiber：无缺陷 Drawn Glass

玻璃稳定 (Si-O 键很强) 但 hydrofluoric acid attacks the Si-O network directly

## Carbon

**Allotropes** 同素异形体：Same **element**, Different structure

<sub>6</sub>Carbon: **4 valence electrons**, 4 covalent bonds per atom.

- Diamond: sp<sup>3</sup>-Tetrahedral **3D Isotropic** Network

  4 strong sigma bonds of 109.5° angle. **无自由电子-insulator 绝缘体**

  No weak planes (通常 defectless)

  钻石在 STP 下亚稳定 (石墨稳定) **metastable**，热力学 thermodynamics 上有转向石墨的倾向，但动力学 kinetics 不允许 (activation barrier too high)。

- Graphite: sp<sup>2</sup>-Trigonal Planar, **3D** Stacked Planes (sheets), **Anisotropic**

  石墨烯层内 hexagonal: 3 strong sigma bonds of 120° angle + **离域大派键 delocalized pi electrons** = **conductive**: For makng Electrodes 电极

  层间只有微弱范德华力 **Van der Waals Forces**，Easy to **slide and slip**: For making **pencil lead** (More clay Harder-H, less clay Softer-B), using pencil is peeling graphene sheets onto paper.

  **Anisotropy**: within sheet 金属导电性，高导热性，高强度；across sheets 弱导电导热强度。

- Graphene 石墨烯: **2D** Single atom **layer of Graphite**

  Stronger than steel by weight, Flexible, Transparent. 最强导电导热体

- Carbon Nanotubes (CNT): **1D** rolled Graphene into seamless cylinder

  High **tensile strength**

- Fullerenes 富勒烯: **0D** C<sub>60</sub>, C<sub>70 </sub>... sp<sup>2</sup> Nanotube caps

  C60: 12 Pentagons + 20 Hexagons

- Amorphous carbon: sp2+sp3 mix, E.g. Soot 烟灰, Charcoal.

- Activated carbon: sp2+sp3 mix

  **Porous**: 1g have surface area of teenis court.

  Absorb odor, pollutant & color: water filter, mask air filters, CO<sub>2</sub> capture



Utilization: supercapacitors, fuel cells, hydrogen storage, high strength carbon fibers for wind turbines, airplanes etc, graphene flexible screens, CNT sensors, tire rubber (strengthened by carbon), lithium-ion batteries...
